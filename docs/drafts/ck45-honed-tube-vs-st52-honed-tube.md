# CK45 Honed Tube vs ST52 Honed Tube: What to Review Before a Hydraulic Cylinder Order

For a hydraulic cylinder buyer, the question is rarely just whether to purchase a CK45 honed tube or an ST52 honed tube. The more useful question is whether the proposed tube, bore condition and machining plan match the complete cylinder requirement. Material naming is important, but it is only one part of the review.

Both CK45 and ST52 are commonly discussed in hydraulic cylinder supply chains. They can be considered for cylinder barrel and precision tube projects, subject to the design, machining route, sealing system and required documentation. A responsible quotation should confirm the drawing first, then identify what is fixed and what must be checked before production.

This guide explains how to compare the two material options without relying on generic claims or assumptions that may not apply to a specific cylinder.

## Start with the cylinder drawing, not the material label

A tube order can look simple when it is described only as “CK45 honed tube” or “ST52 honed tube.” In reality, the cylinder drawing may also define bore and outside diameter, finished length, wall condition, machining allowance, end preparation, weld preparation, surface protection, inspection points and packing expectations. Those requirements can affect material availability, the preparation route and the inspection plan.

Before asking a supplier to recommend CK45 or ST52, provide the current drawing where possible. If the project is based on a sample, provide measured dimensions, clear photographs of the relevant interfaces and an explanation of the end use. A supplier can then review the tube as one part of the cylinder rather than as an isolated length of steel.

The first review should separate three categories of information:

- **Fixed requirements:** dimensions, tolerances, specified material, interfaces and contractual inspection points shown on the drawing.
- **Application information:** equipment type, movement cycle, pressure concept, operating environment, seal system and service expectations.
- **Open production choices:** cut length, packing format, material proposal where the drawing permits alternatives, and the order-specific inspection plan.

This separation prevents a quotation from quietly changing a requirement that should have remained fixed.

## Compare the intended manufacturing route

The tube does not become suitable merely because the material grade has been named. The production route should be discussed in terms the buyer can verify. For a honed tube order, that normally means material preparation, bore finishing, dimensional control, inspection and protected packing.

Ask how the internal bore requirement will be handled. The conversation should cover the requested bore dimension, tolerance, surface condition and the measurement method to be used for final inspection. If the tube will be machined further by the buyer, state the expected machining allowance and identify the features that will be added after delivery. If the supplier will perform end machining or other preparation, those features should appear on the approved drawing or order record.

## Clarify what “honed” means in the purchase order

“Honed” is often used as a broad description, but a purchase order needs a more precise definition. Buyers should state the bore-related requirements that matter for the assembly. This may include internal diameter, tolerance, straightness requirement, finished length, surface condition and any agreed acceptance method.

If the final cylinder uses seals, the tube bore should be reviewed together with the piston, seal design and rod condition. The tube and rod are separate components, but they work in the same assembly. A problem that appears later as inconsistent motion or premature seal wear can be harder to investigate if the original material and finishing requirements were not recorded clearly.

Do not ask a supplier to promise a result that is not tied to an agreed method of verification. Instead, agree on the controlled values, any required test record and the timing of inspection. That approach gives both parties a reference point when the order is being prepared, inspected and packed.

## Review CK45 and ST52 in the context of the application

CK45 and ST52 should be evaluated against the mechanical design and manufacturing requirement, not used as interchangeable wording. The cylinder designer or responsible engineer should confirm the specified grade. When a material alternative is considered, request a written review against the part drawing and intended process.

Useful questions include:

1. Is the grade fixed by the customer drawing, specification or an existing approved part?
2. Will the tube be welded, machined, threaded or otherwise processed after delivery?
3. Is there an identified material certificate or document requirement for the order?
4. Which dimensions and bore conditions are critical to the assembly?
5. Does the application introduce environmental, corrosion, temperature or handling conditions that need to be recorded?

## Plan inspection before the material is released

Inspection is more useful when it follows a defined plan. For a tube order, the plan should describe what will be checked, when it will be checked and which items will be communicated to the buyer. Typical points may include incoming material identification, dimensional check, bore condition review, cut-length verification, visual condition and final packing inspection.

The exact list should follow the order. A standard stock purchase may need a different record from a drawing-based cylinder component order. If a buyer expects documents, labels, photographs, sample approval or a third-party inspection arrangement, those needs should be confirmed before the production schedule is set.

## Do not leave packing until the final day

Tube protection is part of product quality during delivery. The selected packing method should protect the bore and outer surface against handling, moisture, contact damage and shipment movement. The right arrangement depends on cut length, bundle format, delivery route and destination handling conditions.

Buyers should state whether they need identification labels, protective wrapping, separators, export packing or special document references. If the order will be received by a machining facility rather than the final equipment builder, include the handling information needed by that receiving team. A clear packing instruction helps maintain the condition verified at final inspection through to the next process.

## A concise request-for-quotation checklist

When requesting a CK45 or ST52 honed tube quotation, provide as many of the following items as are available:

- Current drawing or sample details
- Proposed material grade and whether alternatives can be considered
- Internal diameter, outside diameter, length and tolerance requirements
- Bore condition and acceptance method
- Downstream machining, welding or assembly information
- Quantity or annual volume expectation
- Inspection record, marking and packing requirements
- Delivery destination and required export documents

This information allows the supplier to prepare a quotation around the real order rather than a generic catalogue description.

## Final perspective

CK45 and ST52 honed tube discussions work best when the material decision is connected to the complete cylinder requirement. A clear drawing review, agreed bore condition, defined inspection plan and practical packing instruction are more valuable than an unsupported general claim about one material being universally better than the other.
